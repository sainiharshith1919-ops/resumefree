About Liftoff

Liftoff exists for one specific problem: most resume advice assumes you already have a job history to fix. If you're a student, a recent graduate, or switching fields, that advice doesn't apply — so this tool reads your projects, coursework, and internships as real evidence, and reviews you by fresher standards instead of penalizing you for not having years of paid work.

FAQ

Is this free to use?

Yes, up to 10 resumes per account. After that, Pro removes the limit and unlocks a few extra tools — see Pricing.

Is my data stored anywhere?

Form details stay in your browser as a draft. Nothing is sent to a server beyond the AI call needed to write, score, and — for Pro tools — extend your resume.

Does it work for any field, not just tech?

Yes — it reads your education, skills and projects to work out what you're suited for, and the job posting box lets you tailor it further to a specific role.

Can I download the resume it writes?

Yes — copy the text, download it as a .txt file, or use Print / Save as PDF from the review panel.

I have no work experience at all — can I still make a good resume?

Yes. Academic projects, coursework, volunteer work, and extracurriculars all count as real evidence for an entry-level resume — that's exactly what this tool is built to surface, instead of expecting a job history you don't have yet.

How long should a fresher's resume be?

One page. Recruiters typically spend only a few seconds on a first look, so a tight, focused page beats a long one.

Should I still write a cover letter?

It's worth it when a posting allows one — it's a chance to explain things a resume can't, and it signals real interest in the specific role. The Pro cover-letter tool can draft a starting point from your resume.

What skills matter most on a fresher resume?

A mix of technical skills specific to the role plus a few soft skills employers consistently rank highly for entry-level hires — communication, reliability, and adaptability, alongside whatever hard skills the job actually needs.

Should I include my GPA or academic marks?

If they're strong and recent, yes — it's common practice for freshers. If they're a weak point, lead with projects and skills instead, and this tool's review will tell you when that trade-off makes sense.
